Regional Digital Public Infrastructure and Command Control Center for Human-Nature Security and Resilience in Africa

Japan research-academia partnership betwen RESTEC (Remi Chandran) and Fukushima University (Philip McCasland, Maksym Gusyev, and Yuliia Bezhenar) institutions

Overview

Africa faces interconnected environmental, climate and human-security challenges that increasingly cross national and institutional boundaries. Human–wildlife conflict, wildlife trafficking, agro-pastoral conflict, land-use change, climate impacts and other environmental pressures require timely information and coordinated responses across borders.

However, the information needed to understand and respond to these challenges is often fragmented across institutions, sectors and countries. The absence of integrated information systems can limit the ability of governments and regional institutions to undertake timely risk assessment, monitoring, early warning and coordinated action.

The challenges of a fragmented data system is that, the information required for timely data sharing for addressing human-nature conflicts do not reach the stakeholders or communities. This could be lead loss of human life as well as destruction of nature.

About

The proposed Regional Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) for Human–Nature Security and Resilience in Africa seeks to address this gap by establishing a common digital foundation for integrating and sharing spatial and non-spatial information across institutional and national boundaries.

The initiative brings together:

Earth observation

Geospatial information

Ground-based observations


...and other institutional datasets within a trusted and interoperable digital environment.
By connecting these sources of information, the Regional DPI is intended to support evidence-based decision-making, strengthen regional cooperation and improve the capacity of institutions to anticipate and respond to emerging human–nature and environmental risks

Goal

The ultimate aim is to move from fragmented information and isolated responses towards connected regional intelligence and coordinated action — strengthening both human security and ecological resilience across Africa.

The initiative is envisaged as a public-interest regional infrastructure, supporting collaboration among African governments, regional organisations, United Nations entities, research institutions and technology partners. A proposed regional operational and Command-and-Control Centre in Nairobi would provide an institutional hub for information integration, analysis, coordination and decision support.
